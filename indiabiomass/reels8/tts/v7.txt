Four close-ups. Guess what each one is. The answer comes after each.
Number one. Fine, golden and light. It comes off the grain at a rice mill.
Rice husk. A common boiler fuel, because it is dry and already collected in one place.
Number two. Pale and fibrous. It is what is left after the juice is pressed out of sugarcane.
Bagasse. Many sugar mills burn it to power themselves.
Number three. Light, papery pieces, left after threshing a winter oilseed.
Mustard husk. Common across Rajasthan.
Number four. Hard, curved and brown. Mostly from the South.
Coconut shell. Dense, and also made into charcoal.
