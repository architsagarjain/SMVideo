India does not have one kind of biomass. It has a different one in almost every region.
In Punjab and Haryana, it is paddy straw, left behind after the rice harvest.
In Rajasthan, mustard husk.
Across Gujarat and Maharashtra, cotton stalks and groundnut shells.
In Uttar Pradesh and Maharashtra, bagasse from the sugar mills.
In the South, coconut shells. And rice husk, wherever rice is milled.
Every one of these is a fuel, if it reaches the right buyer.
The problem is distance. The straw is in one state, the factory that needs it is in another, and neither knows the other exists.
IndiaBiomass is mapping who has what, and where, so supply and demand can finally find each other.
